# Belgium and Flanders

Research in Belgium is close to French practice in form and far from it in
access rules, languages and the one record France never had: the
continuous population register. Read this when a line crosses into Belgium
or when a Belgian-born ancestor appears in a French record.

## Jurisdictions and dates that matter

| Period | Regime | Consequence for records |
| ------ | ------ | ----------------------- |
| to 1795 | Austrian Netherlands (Flanders, Brabant, Hainaut, Namur, Luxembourg), Prince-Bishopric of Liège, some French enclaves | Parish registers only; Liège and its enclaves have their own series |
| 1795-1814 | French départements (Lys, Escaut, Deux-Nèthes, Dyle, Jemmapes, Sambre-et-Meuse, Ourthe, Forêts, Meuse-Inférieure) | État civil from 1796 (an IV) in French, Republican calendar; French conscription; enregistrement and hypothèques |
| 1815-1830 | United Kingdom of the Netherlands | Civil acts in Dutch in Flanders, French in Wallonia; militia registers |
| 1830- | Kingdom of Belgium; provinces roughly the old départements | Civil registration continues; population registers from 1846 |
| 1919 | Eupen-Malmedy annexed from Germany | German Standesamt records before, Belgian after |
| 1977 | Communal mergers (2 359 → 596 communes) | Records held by the merged commune or deposited at the State Archives |

Belgian provinces: Antwerp, East Flanders, West Flanders, Limburg, Flemish
Brabant (Dutch); Hainaut, Liège, Luxembourg, Namur, Walloon Brabant
(French, German in the east); Brussels bilingual. The commune (gemeente)
holds the state, as in France.

## Languages and names

- Parish registers: Latin, sometimes Dutch or French. Civil acts: French
  1796-1814 everywhere, then Dutch in Flanders (with French persisting in
  some towns until the 1930s), French in Wallonia.
- Given names are Latinised in parish registers (Joannes, Petrus,
  Maria Theresia) and appear in the vernacular in civil acts (Jan, Pieter,
  Marie-Thérèse); the same person is Joannes Baptista, Jan Baptist and
  Jean-Baptiste across records.
- Flemish surnames keep their spelling better than French ones after 1796
  but vary before: Van de Velde / Vandevelde / Van der Velde, De Smet /
  De Smedt / Desmet; prefixes (Van, De, Ver-) are part of the surname and
  are indexed under V or D in Belgian practice, under the main word in
  Dutch practice.
- Women appear under their birth surname in every Belgian record, which
  helps; addresses and "huisvrouw van" (wife of) are frequent.

## Record types by period

| Record | Dates | Content and value |
| ------ | ----- | ----------------- |
| Parochieregisters / registres paroissiaux | c. 1580-1796 (some from the 1560s) | Baptisms with godparents, marriages with witnesses, burials; Latin; well preserved and fully online |
| Burgerlijke stand / état civil | 1796- | French model; decennial tables (tienjarige tafels) from 1803 |
| Bevolkingsregisters / registres de population | 1846- (some towns from 1800-1815, Ghent 1796) | Continuous household register: see below |
| Militieregisters / registres de milice | 1815- | Conscription lists by commune and year of birth + 19; description, exemption, substitute |
| Notarial minutes | 16th c.- | State Archives by notary; indexed in part on search.arch.be |
| Successierechten (succession duties) | 1817- | Death, heirs, estate; at the State Archives |
| Vreemdelingendossiers / dossiers étrangers | 1835- | Files on foreigners in Belgium (Sûreté publique), at the State Archives; the mirror of the French files on Belgians |
| Kadaster | 1834- | Owners and parcels (primitive cadastre from the French period) |
| Staten van goed | 15th-18th c. (Flanders) | Estate inventories after a death with minor heirs; kinship spelled out, the pre-1796 gold mine for Flemish families |
| Poorterboeken | to 1795 | Registers of new burghers of a town, with origin |
| Wezenregisters | to 1795 | Orphan chamber records naming guardians and kin |

## Population registers: the key record

From 1846 (formally; many communes earlier), each commune kept a register
of every household, updated continuously between censuses: every member
with birth date and place, occupation, civil status, relationship to the
head, date of arrival and where from, date of departure and where to, or
date of death. A person can be followed house by house and commune by
commune, and a departure "naar Frankrijk" or "vers la France" with a
destination commune is the record that links a Belgian family to its
French descendants. Registers are kept by decade (1846-1856, 1856-1866,
1866-1880, 1880-1890, 1890-1900, 1900-1910, 1910-1920, 1920-1930,
1930-1947). Indexes (alphabetical tables) exist per volume. Held by the
commune or the State Archives; large towns have them online (Antwerp,
Ghent, Bruges, Leuven, Mechelen, Brussels communes), others via
FamilySearch images or in the reading room.

## Access rules

Since the 2018 law (in force 2019): births public after 100 years,
marriages after 75, deaths after 50. Online images stop at those limits.
The population registers follow the same limits per volume. For recent
acts, descendants apply to the commune with proof of filiation. Notarial
minutes: 75 years.

## From France back to Belgium

Belgians were the largest foreign population in France through the 19th
century: Flemish agricultural and textile workers in the Nord (Lille,
Roubaix, Tourcoing, Halluin), seasonal workers in the Aisne and Oise,
Walloons in the Ardennes, and the whole Franco-Belgian border region
where families lived on both sides. To find the Belgian origin:

1. The French marriage or death act usually gives the birthplace; for
   Flemish names it is often misspelled (Meenen for Menen, Courtray for
   Kortrijk, Ypres for Ieper). Use the Dutch and French names of the
   commune when searching.
2. French *dossiers de naturalisation* (Archives nationales BB/11) and the
   *registres d'immatriculation des étrangers* (AD Nord, series M, from
   1888) give the exact birthplace and parents.
3. The Belgian population register of the birthplace shows the departure
   with the destination, confirming the identity.
4. The Belgian *militieregister* of the class (birth year + 19) lists the
   young man even if he had already left, with the note "in Frankrijk".
5. The Belgian succession declaration of a parent lists the children
   abroad with their residence.

## Brick-wall specifics

- **Latin to Dutch to French names**: search all three forms of the given
  name and the prefix-joined and prefix-split surname.
- **Border parishes**: Flemish-speaking parishes of French Flanders
  (Hazebrouck, Bailleul, Dunkirk area) have registers in Latin and Dutch
  in French archives; Belgian border communes have French acts. Search
  both countries' archives for the same village.
- **Staten van goed** replace missing marriages and give the children of a
  couple with ages before 1796.
- **Poorterboeken** give the origin of a townsman who arrived from the
  countryside.
- **Foundlings** of Antwerp, Ghent and Brussels: hospice registers
  (Maagdenhuis, Kinderen Alyns, Bijstand), surnames invented.
- **Communal mergers**: the record may be under the pre-1977 commune name
  in FamilySearch and under the new one at the State Archives.
- **Eupen-Malmedy and the German-speaking east**: Prussian records
  (Standesamt from 1874, Kirchenbücher) before 1920; see the Poland and
  Prussia reference for their form.
