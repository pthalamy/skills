## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in descending order of evidentiary weight:

1. A contemporaneous original civil or church register act.
2. A certified or archival copy, with the repository and image/page reference.
3. A later act that explicitly identifies the person or parents.
4. An independently sourced census, military, notarial, cemetery, or institutional record.
5. A public genealogy tree with visible source references.
6. An unsourced tree, index, name search, or user assertion.

The hierarchy is a starting point, not an automatic verdict. An act can concern the wrong person; compare age, occupation, residence, witnesses, spouse, and parents.

## Conflicts

Preserve both claims when they cannot yet be resolved. Add a note that states:

- the conflicting values;
- the records supporting each value;
- why one currently appears stronger, if applicable;
- the concrete next record or archive search that could decide it.

Do not overwrite a sourced value with an unsourced value. Do not delete the weaker claim merely because it is inconvenient for a tree layout.

## Uncertain transcriptions

Transcribe legible text literally. For ambiguous handwriting, retain the best reading and document alternatives, for example: `Marie Louise "Lison" [Lisou?]`. Record whether the uncertainty concerns a given name, surname, date, place, or relationship. Keep editorial expansions separate from the literal transcription.

## Research notes

Use notes for provenance and reasoning, not as a substitute for a relationship pointer. A useful note includes the record type, date, place, archive/site, image or page, URL, and what the record proves. Mark a proposed connection as `HYPOTHESIS` until a source establishes it.
