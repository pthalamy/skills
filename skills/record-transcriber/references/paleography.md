## Letterforms and habits

- **Long s** (`ſ`) looks like an `f` without the crossbar; `ſ` and `s`
  alternate in the same word (`Franſois`, `paroiſſe`). Transcribe as `s`.
- **u / v and i / j** are interchangeable before the 18th century: `Iean`
  is `Jean`, `vn` is `un`, `Iulliet` is `Juillet`. Transcribe as written in
  `literal`; normalise in the structured fields.
- **Double letters** are often marked with a single letter and a tilde or
  a stroke above: `cõmune`, `hõme`.
- **Final flourishes** on `-s`, `-e`, `-r` are decorative, not letters.
- **Capital letters** were used freely; do not infer a proper name from a
  capital alone.
- **e / o / a** in cursive are told apart by the loop; **n / u** and
  **m / in** are often identical: use the word's context (`Martin` vs
  `Martiu`) and mark `[?]` when the word is a name that could be either.
- **y** carries a dot in some hands, **i** carries none in others.
- **Numerals**: `1` can look like `j`, `7` like `1` with a foot, `4` open
  like `y`; 17th-century dates are almost always in words.
- Many 18th-century hands write `-oit` where modern French has `-ait`
  (`estoit`, `françois` for *français*). Keep as written.

## Numbers, dates and hours in words

Dates before 1792 and most civil dates until the mid-19th century are
written in full: `le vingt sixiesme jour de febvrier mil six cent
soixante dix huit`. Watch for:

- `mil` for *mille*; `sept cens`, `six cens` for *cents*.
- `quatre vingt(s)`, `soixante dix`, and in the east and Savoy `septante`,
  `octante`/`huitante`, `nonante`.
- `l'an de grâce`, `l'an du Seigneur`, and the regnal formula `l'an … du
  règne de …` in some notarial acts.
- Hours: `à sept heures du soir`, `sur les onze heures`, `à minuit`.
- Ages: `âgé de … ans`, `ou environ`, `à peu près`, `passé`, `révolus`;
  `majeur`/`mineur` (majority was 25 until 1792, 21 from 1792 to 1974).
- Ordinals: `premier`, `deuxiesme`; `vingt et un`, `trente un`.

The act date is the date the words describe; do not shift it for a
Julian-to-Gregorian difference in France after December 1582.

## French Republican calendar

In force 22 September 1792 to 31 December 1805 (1 vendémiaire I to 10
nivôse XIV). Months of 30 days, then 5 or 6 *jours complémentaires*
(sans-culottides).

| Month | Abbrev. | Starts (years II to VII) |
| ----- | ------- | ------------------------ |
| vendémiaire | VEND | 22 Sep |
| brumaire | BRUM | 22 Oct |
| frimaire | FRIM | 21 Nov |
| nivôse | NIVO | 21 Dec |
| pluviôse | PLUV | 20 Jan |
| ventôse | VENT | 19 Feb |
| germinal | GERM | 21 Mar |
| floréal | FLOR | 20 Apr |
| prairial | PRAI | 20 May |
| messidor | MESS | 19 Jun |
| thermidor | THER | 19 Jul |
| fructidor | FRUC | 18 Aug |
| jours complémentaires | COMP | 17 Sep |

Start dates shift by a day around sextile years (III, VII, XI) and from
year VIII onward; use `scripts/transcription_to_gedcom.py date` for exact
conversion rather than the table. Years are written in Roman numerals or
words (`an quatre`, `an IV`, `l'an 4e de la République française une et
indivisible`). Décadi, primidi, duodi… name the day of the 10-day week
and are not needed for the date. Some acts of years I and II carry both
calendars.

## Formulas: parish registers in French

Slots in brackets are what to extract.

**Baptism** (16th c. to 1792):

> Le [date] a esté baptisé(e) [prénom], né(e) [le même jour / hier / le
> …], fils/fille de [père], [profession], et de [mère] sa femme, de
> [paroisse / hameau]. Le parrain a esté [nom], [profession, lien], la
> marraine [nom], [lien], lesquels ont signé / déclaré ne sçavoir signer.

Variants: `fils naturel` (illegitimate), `de père inconnu`, `né de
légitime mariage`, `ondoyé` (emergency baptism at home, later
`suppléé les cérémonies`), `enfant trouvé / exposé`.

**Marriage**:

> Le [date], après la publication des trois bans faite [dates] sans
> opposition [ou: avec dispense de deux bans], [et dispense de parenté au
> …e degré obtenue de Monseigneur], ont receu la bénédiction nuptiale
> [époux], [âge], [profession], fils de [père] et de [mère] [vivants /
> défunts], de la paroisse de […], d'une part, et [épouse], [âge], fille
> de […], d'autre part, en présence de [témoins avec liens].

Watch `veuf/veuve de` (previous spouse named), `du consentement de`,
`avec le consentement de leurs parents`, `majeur(e) d'ans`.

**Burial**:

> Le [date] a esté inhumé(e) dans le cimetière / l'église de ce lieu le
> corps de [nom], [âge], [profession], [époux / veuve de …], décédé(e)
> [hier / le …] [muni des sacrements], en présence de [témoins].

A burial act rarely gives parents for an adult; for a child it gives the
father and often the mother.

## Formulas: parish registers in Latin

Latin persists in some dioceses into the 18th century and in Alsace,
Savoy, Nice, Corsica and neighbouring countries longer.

- Baptism: `Anno Domini [annus] die [dies] mensis [mensis] baptizatus /
  baptizata fuit [nomen] filius / filia legitimus / legitima [pater] et
  [mater] conjugum [loci]. Patrinus fuit [nomen], matrina [nomen].`
  `natus / nata heri / eodem die / pridie`; `illegitimus`, `spurius`,
  `ex patre incognito`.
- Marriage: `matrimonio juncti sunt in facie ecclesiae`, `tribus
  denuntiationibus praemissis`, `nullo detecto impedimento`, `dispensatione
  obtenta in [tertio] gradu consanguinitatis / affinitatis`, `praesentibus
  testibus`.
- Burial: `sepultus / sepulta est in coemeterio`, `obiit`, `defunctus`,
  `sacramentis munitus`, `aetatis suae annorum [n] circiter`, `viduus /
  vidua`.
- Kinship: `filius`, `filia`, `uxor` (wife), `maritus`, `relicta` (widow),
  `conjuges`, `avus`, `avia`, `patruus` (paternal uncle), `avunculus`
  (maternal uncle), `consobrinus` (cousin), `nepos` (grandson or nephew).
- Names are declined: `Petri` is the genitive of *Petrus* and means "of
  Pierre"; `Joannae` of *Joanna*. Give the nominative in the normalised
  field and the French form only if the parish was French-speaking.

## Formulas: civil registration

**1792 to 1803**: free form, Republican dates, the officer is the
*officier public* of the commune, witnesses (two for a birth, four for a
marriage) with ages, occupations and residences. Ages in years often give
`ans` only.

**Code civil (1804) onward**, the classic structure:

> L'an [année], le [jour] du mois de [mois], à [heure] heures du [matin /
> soir], par devant nous [nom], maire / adjoint, officier de l'état civil
> de la commune de […], canton de […], département de […], est comparu
> [déclarant], âgé de [n] ans, [profession], domicilié à […], lequel nous a
> présenté un enfant du sexe [masculin / féminin], né [hier / ce jour] à
> [heure] en son domicile / en la maison de …, de lui déclarant et de
> [mère], son épouse, âgée de …, [profession], et auquel il a déclaré
> vouloir donner les prénoms de […]. Lesdites déclaration et présentation
> faites en présence de [témoin 1], âgé de …, [profession], domicilié à …,
> et de [témoin 2] …, et ont [le père et les témoins] signé avec nous le
> présent acte, après lecture faite. [ou: ont déclaré ne savoir signer]

**Marriage**: publications (dates, places), the parties with birth date
and place, occupation, residence, parents (names, occupations, residence,
`ici présents et consentants` or `décédé(e)`, with dates and places of
death cited from produced acts), previous marriages, *actes respectueux*
for parents refusing consent, marriage contract (`contrat reçu par Me …
notaire à …, le …`, mandatory mention from 1850), children legitimised,
four witnesses with kinship (`oncle de l'époux`, `ami des époux`).

**Death**: declarants (two until 1919, then one) and their relationship
(`voisin`, `fils du défunt`, `employé des pompes funèbres`); the deceased's
age, birth place and date, occupation, residence, parents (names, alive or
dead, from about 1823 more systematically), spouse (`époux de`, `veuf de`).
Information about the deceased is secondary; take it with the informant's
relationship in mind.

## Marginal mentions

Written in the margin later, each with its own date and authority. From
1897 (marriage, divorce), 1917 (recognition, legitimation), 1945 (death on
the birth act), plus rectification judgments and adoptions at any date.
Typical text: `Marié à [commune] le [date] avec [nom]`, `Divorcé par
jugement du tribunal de […] du […], transcrit le […]`, `Décédé à […] le
[…]`, `Reconnu par acte du […]`, `Rectifié par jugement du […]`. Each is a
distinct event with its own citation; do not fold it into the main act.

## Status, kinship and occupation vocabulary

- `feu`, `feue`, `défunt(e)`: deceased at the act date, so `DEAT BEF`
  act date. `vivant(e)` says the opposite explicitly.
- `veuf / veuve de`: names a prior spouse; `en premières / secondes noces`.
- `fils / fille de` followed by both parents; `et de feue` marks only the
  mother as dead.
- `dit`, `dite`: a by-name, `Pierre Martin dit Lafleur`; record as an
  alias, not part of the surname.
- `demoiselle`, `dame`, `sieur`, `honorable homme`, `noble`, `écuyer`,
  `messire`: social rank, not occupation.
- `laboureur` (owns a plough team) versus `manœuvre`, `journalier`,
  `brassier` (day labourers); `ménager`, `cultivateur`, `propriétaire`,
  `fermier`, `métayer`, `closier`, `bordier`: record as written, they are
  not synonyms.
- `natif de`, `originaire de`: birthplace; `demeurant à`, `domicilié à`,
  `habitant`: residence. Keep them separate.
- `parents` in old French can mean relatives, not father and mother;
  `cousin` is loose; `beau-frère` may mean brother-in-law or stepbrother.

## Signatures

At the foot: `ont signé`, `a signé avec nous`, `a déclaré ne savoir signer
de ce interpellé` (after 1804), `ont fait leur marque`. Record for each
person: signed, made a mark, declared unable, or not stated. A signature
can spell the surname differently from the act body and is usually the
better authority for the family's own spelling. A person who signs in one
act and marks in a later one is probably a different person.

## Transcription conventions

- Line breaks of the original preserved in `literal`; number lines in the
  reply for reference.
- `[abc]` expansion of an abbreviation; `[?]` doubtful preceding word;
  `[abc|abd]` alternatives, most likely first; `[...]` illegible, with
  `[... 3 mots]` when the length is estimable; `[sic]` after an evident
  slip; `<abc>` text struck through in the original; `\abc/` text inserted
  above the line; `{margin: ...}` marginal text at its position.
- Do not add accents, apostrophes or modern punctuation that the scribe
  did not write. `estoit`, `sçavoir`, `febvrier` stay.
- Proper names keep their original spelling in `literal` and in the
  `as_written` fields; the `normalised` fields carry the modern or
  canonical form the researcher will use.
